Esports betting is a relatively young but fast-growing betting niche. It has
several features that set it apart from betting on traditional sports.

## What makes esports different

- **High event frequency** — tournaments run almost year-round, and you can
  find matches on practically any day of the week.
- **The impact of patches and updates** — game balance can shift after each
  new update, which strongly affects team statistics.
- **Rosters change more often** — player transfers happen far more frequently
  than in traditional sports and have a major effect on results.

## Popular disciplines for betting

1. **Dota 2** — long Bo3/Bo5 formats where draft analytics matter a great
   deal.
2. **CS2 (Counter-Strike)** — maps and team lineups heavily influence the
   outcome, and betting on individual maps within a series is popular.
3. **League of Legends** — regional leagues and international tournaments
   with their own meta-analysis built around patches.
4. **Valorant** — a comparatively new discipline with a growing number of
   markets offered by bookmakers.

## What beginners should pay attention to

- Study not just a team's overall statistics but its current roster — a
  single player change can radically shift a team's level.
- Keep an eye on the patch calendar — sudden balance changes can temporarily
  make historical statistics less meaningful.
- Remember the bookmaker's margin: esports odds often vary between operators
  far more than odds on popular traditional sports.

## The takeaway

Esports betting demands a deeper understanding of the discipline's context
than betting on "classic" sports — and suits those who are ready to follow
the news and patches on a regular basis.
